The story

The name comes from two things that mark summer in the yard: the first warm week of June, and the ferns that take over the north side of the house. The first prints were on tea towels, then an old chambray shirt that was too good to give away. People kept asking where it came from.

Everything is still made by one pair of hands. Garments are thrifted or passed along, washed twice, mended if they need it, then coated and dried in a dark closet. On a clear day they go out on the lawn under glass, with whatever is in season laid on top. Fifteen minutes later they come inside for a long rinse, and the blue appears.

The shop is small on purpose. Pieces go up a handful at a time, usually on a Saturday morning. Sold pieces stay on the site because the whole body of work matters more than the inventory.

Why cyanotype

Cyanotype was invented in 1842 and was how engineers copied drawings for a century, which is where the word blueprint comes from. Anna Atkins used it a year later to make the first book illustrated with photographs: a catalogue of British algae, white on blue.

It is also one of the gentlest processes there is. No silver, no darkroom, no solvents. The only chemistry is two iron salts and sunlight, and what rinses out goes down the drain as diluted iron. The blue that remains, Prussian blue, is the same pigment painters have used since the 1700s.

Caring for a print

  • Wash cold, inside out, with a mild soap. No bleach, no oxygen boosters, no phosphate detergents: they will strip the blue.
  • Line dry in the shade. The pigment is lightfast but years of direct noon sun will soften anything.
  • The blue deepens over the first few washes and settles after that. A little unevenness, a halo around a leaf, a brush mark at the edge: that is the print.
  • Iron on low if you must, on the reverse side.

Shipping & returns

Orders ship from Northeast Ohio within 3 business days, USPS with tracking. Flat rate shipping in the United States, free over $150. You will get an email with the tracking number the moment the parcel is scanned, and you can follow it from the track your order page any time.

Because every piece is one of a kind, sales are final. If something arrives damaged or not as described, write within 7 days with a photo and we will make it right, whether that is a repair, a replacement from the next batch, or a refund.

International shipping is not open yet. If you are outside the US and in love with something, write and we will work it out by hand.
